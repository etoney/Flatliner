===============================================================================
  FLATLINER  //  CONTROLS QUICK REFERENCE
  Castle Danger Music Company
===============================================================================

Flatliner is an automatic fader rider. It listens to your vocal and moves a
virtual fader for you, pushing quiet words up and pulling loud ones down, so
the performance sits on one flat line: the TARGET.

Tip: double-click a knob to reset it. Double-click the value under a knob to
type an exact number (e.g. "-18" for the target).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
RIDE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  RIDE          The fader moves by itself.
  OFF           The fader is parked at 0 dB and Flatliner does nothing (the
                OUTPUT trim still works).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE KNOBS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  TARGET        The level the rider aims for (measured as RMS, so it follows
                how loud the vocal FEELS, not the peaks). Start around -18 dB,
                then raise or lower it until the vocal sits where you want it
                in the mix. Used when SECTIONS is off.

  SECTIONS      (under TARGET, on by default) Flatliner aims at where the
                vocal has been sitting for the last few seconds instead of one
                level for the whole song. It still evens out loud and soft
                words, but a chorus sung louder stays louder and a quiet verse
                stays quiet. THE LINE shows the moving SECTION line. Turn it
                off for one fixed level (TARGET), e.g. a podcast or voice-over.

  RANGE         The most the fader is allowed to move, up or down. Small
                (2-4 dB) = a gentle, invisible ride. Large (10-18 dB) =
                flattens everything, including whispers and shouts.

  SPEED         How fast the fader moves. Low = slow and natural, like a
                patient engineer. High = locked flat. Cuts always move faster
                than boosts, like a human hand on a fader.

  GATE          Below this level the fader freezes where it is, so breaths,
                room noise and the gaps between phrases are not pushed up.
                If the fader pumps noise up in the pauses, raise the gate.

  LOOKAHEAD     Delays the audio (0-50 ms) while the rider listens to it
                early, so the fader is already moving when a loud word or a
                whisper arrives, instead of reacting after it. Flatliner tells
                your DAW about the delay, and the DAW lines everything up.
                Set it to 0 (OFF) for live tracking, where you can't have any
                latency. The LATENCY screen shows what is being reported.
                Tip: set it once and leave it. Some DAWs only pick up a
                latency change when you restart playback.

  MUSIC         Needs your backing track routed to Flatliner's SIDECHAIN
                input. The louder the music gets, the higher the vocal TARGET
                is pushed (up to +12 dB at 100%), so the vocal rides on top
                of the mix instead of getting buried. 0% = ignore the music.
                The MUSIC LINK screen shows whether a sidechain is connected.

  OUTPUT        Final output trim (-12 to +12 dB).


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THE SCREENS
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE LINE      A scrolling scope of the last ten seconds.
                  dim trace    = the vocal as it comes in
                  bright trace = after the fader (this should be flat)
                  yellow dashes = TARGET      shaded band = RANGE
                  grey dashes   = GATE
  FADER GAUGE   The round gauge in the header. Needle left = cutting,
                right = boosting, centre = 0 dB.
  METERS        VOICE in, OUT, and the FADER (cuts hang down in red, boosts
                climb up in cream), plus a read-out of the fader in dB.
  PRESETS       Factory presets and your own. Your presets are saved in
                  macOS:    ~/Library/Application Support/Castle Danger/Flatliner/Presets
                  Windows:  %APPDATA%\Castle Danger\Flatliner\Presets


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
GETTING STARTED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1. Put Flatliner on the vocal track, early in the chain (before the
     compressor and effects, so they get an even signal).
  2. Pick "Lead Vocal: Natural" and play the loudest and quietest parts.
  3. Adjust TARGET until the bright trace sits where you want it.
  4. If it feels too busy, lower RANGE or SPEED. If whispers still get lost,
     raise RANGE. If it lifts the noise between phrases, raise GATE.


-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
ARA: FLATLINER ON THE CLIP ITSELF
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  In a DAW with ARA (Studio Pro, Logic, Cubase, Reaper and others), apply
  Flatliner to the vocal clip instead of inserting it on the track. In Studio
  Pro: drag Flatliner from the browser onto the clip (or pick it from the
  clip's right-click menu).

  It then reads the whole take as soon as the DAW lets it (a few seconds,
  without playing), and plans the ride with the whole take in view:
    - SECTIONS are centred on each moment (the 3 s before AND after), and a
      section change is found where it happens, so a chorus has its level
      from its first word.
    - Every move is planned ahead: the fader is already there when the word
      starts. No lookahead, no latency.
    - Breaths and gaps are known: the fader holds through them and glides to
      the next phrase's level just before it starts. It never pumps on
      silence.
  The knobs work as usual (TARGET, RANGE, SPEED, GATE, SECTIONS, RIDE,
  OUTPUT); turning one re-plans at once. LOOKAHEAD isn't needed, and MUSIC
  works in live mode only (on the clip there's no sidechain).

  THE LINE shows the whole take: dim = the take, bright = after the planned
  fader, dashed = where it aims, and a marker where playback is. It says
  READING THE TAKE... until the take has been read. The status screens say
  ARA: ON THE CLIP and NO LATENCY.

  Edit the clip (comp, trim, move) and Flatliner reads it again by itself.
  A clip at a different sample rate from the song plays untouched for now.
  ARA works on clips, so a vocal BUS still needs Flatliner in live mode.
  In a DAW without ARA (Ableton Live), Flatliner is always in live mode.

-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
LINKED TO THE MASTER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  If The Master is on your master bus, Flatliner links to it by itself (the
  line under the logo says LINKED TO THE MASTER). Nothing to set up.

  Flatliner measures how much the track's level swings (its quiet and loud
  parts, and the gaps between phrases) from The Master's RESET onwards. In
  MY PLUGINS mode, The Master finds which track it's on (by track name, or by
  matching it to The Servant on the same track) and on APPLY sets:
    SECTIONS on, so the vocal keeps each section's level
    TARGET   where the lead already sits on average (used if SECTIONS is off)
    RANGE    from the measured swing and the genre, at most 8 dB: it only
             evens out the words
    SPEED    from the genre
    GATE     between the gaps and the quietest words
  RIDE goes on; LOOKAHEAD, MUSIC and OUTPUT are left as you set them. The
  preset box shows "Set by The Master" and every change is explained in The
  Master's WHAT I CHANGED & WHY log. Turn anything afterwards.

  Without Flatliner, The Master still tells you when your lead jumps around
  enough to need it.

REBEL  (next to the link lamp)
  On: The Master keeps its hands off this plugin. Nothing it sends changes a setting here; it still
  hears what the plugin does and plans the rest of the mix around it. Saved with the project; presets
  never change it. Turn it off to let The Master run the plugin again.
